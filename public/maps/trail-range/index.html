How the score works

Each dimension is mapped linearly to a fixed 0–100 scale, then combined using your normalized weights. Values beyond the endpoints are capped. These are analyst-chosen reference ranges, not pass/fail standards. Filtering vehicles never rescales the score.

Score = Σ (normalized weight × dimension score)
Dimension0 points100 points

Defaults: clearance 30%, approach 20%, departure 20%, breakover 20%, tire diameter 10%, wheelbase 0%. Tire diameter and clearance are correlated; wheelbase is already partly reflected in breakover.

What is measured—and what isn't

This index measures static obstacle geometry. It does not model traction, articulation, tire tread, gearing, armor, driver skill, payload, reliability, price, or high-speed desert performance. Use the hardware filter to compare vehicles with the drivetrain equipment you need.

Tires: nominal diameter = rim inches + 2 × tire width in mm × aspect ratio ÷ 25.4. Flotation sizes use their listed outside diameter (for example, 37x12.50R17 → 37 inches). This differs from a loaded tire radius or a rounded “35-inch” label.

Missing breakover: when enabled, β = 2 × atan(2 × clearance ÷ wheelbase), converted to degrees. This assumes a centered underside point and point tire contacts; actual belly geometry and tire contact change the result. It is not a manufacturer measurement.

Vehicles missing published approach or departure angles have been removed. Missing breakover can still use the explicitly labeled estimate. Turn estimates off to require published values for every weighted dimension, or set breakover weight to zero. All-zero weights produce no score or frontier.

Sources and scope

MPG is the EPA combined rating, joined by model year, engine, transmission and configuration. Some EPA records group trims, body styles or tire options; each vehicle's notes identify consequential grouping. These are laboratory ratings, not predictions of your driving.

Manufacturer clearance conventions differ. Load, optional tires, air-suspension height and tow hitches can change dimensions. Every point retains configuration notes and source links; estimates are never silently substituted.
